SMART Greenhouse combines AI, automated irrigation, and solar energy to help a community grow crops while using less water.

The problem

Water is under pressure.
The ability to grow shouldn't be.

Local water supplies are under growing pressure from drought and rising temperatures. In some areas, data centers add to the demand. Communities still need to grow food. SMART Greenhouse uses AI and automated irrigation to help local growers conserve water. It also gives the community a hands-on way to learn how technology supports agriculture.

Purpose & Vision

Build one SMART Greenhouse for a water-limited county. Use AI and automation to conserve water, support healthy crops, and demonstrate how technology can support agriculture.

SMART Objectives

  • Build for year-round crops within 10 months.
  • Integrate 4 core technologies: mobile dashboard, automated irrigation, AI plant monitor, and solar panels.
  • Target at least 30% less water than manual irrigation.

Community & Education

A hands-on STEM resource for schools and community groups. Explore AI and sustainable growing while keeping people in control.

Charter details: success measures, constraints & assumptions

How we will define success

  • Construction and installed systems pass applicable safety tests and final inspection.
  • The dashboard displays connected sensor data and system status.
  • Moisture-based irrigation demonstrates at least 30% lower water use against a manual-watering baseline.
  • AI identifies potential plant-health concerns for review; solar provides power to designated systems.
  • The website is published and operating / maintenance instructions are handed over.

Project constraints

  • 10-month project timeline, targeting an operational greenhouse by completion.
  • No more than 25% of project work may be contracted out.
  • Compliance with local building regulations and applicable safety requirements.

Planning assumptions

Budget and land will be available; permits will be approved on time; contractors and technology components will be available; and the selected community will have a need for the project.

Irrigation Logic & Drip Mechanics

The Fully Automated Irrigation System integrates soil moisture sensors placed strategically across the root zones. When moisture levels drop below the preset threshold (40%), solenoid valves open automatically, delivering targeted drip irrigation directly to plant roots. This prevents over-watering, minimizes evaporation loss in arid environments, and guarantees compliance with our 30% water savings success criteria.

How AI Supports Growers

The AI Plant Health monitoring system demonstrates how artificial intelligence can support sustainable agriculture by helping growers make better decisions, conserve resources, and improve crop health. Rather than replacing human farmers and gardeners, AI acts as an assistant tool that monitors environmental conditions, identifies potential problems early, and provides actionable recommendations.

1

Early Problem Identification

Detects subtle chlorosis or leaf wilting days before visible to the naked eye.

2

Actionable Recommendations

Suggests precise fertilizer or humidity adjustments tailored to specific crop strains.

3

Predicted Harvest

Estimates yield forecasts (~120 kg/cycle), optimal harvest windows (12-14 days), and crop readiness using predictive environmental models.

Resource Planning

Resource Plan & Budget Description

Labor requirements, contractor allocation limits (25% max), and budget allocation.

Labor & Contractor Plan

The project utilizes both internal core team members and specialized external contractors. In strict adherence to project constraints, **only 25% of project work may be contracted out**. Contractors are strictly retained for specialized structural design and heavy greenhouse assembly, while our core team manages software development, AI monitoring integration, and stakeholder education.

Budget Description

The total capital budget is allocated across structural materials (40%), smart sensor hardware & AI integration (25%), solar renewable energy systems (15%), contractor fees (20%, adhering to the 25% work limit), and community contingency reserves. Funding is supported by county grants and environmental organization sponsorships.
